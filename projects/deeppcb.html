01Intro

DeepPCB is InstaDeep's AI-powered, cloud-native PCB routing software. It routes complex boards without requiring the user to be a routing specialist, which is the pitch the site had to land.

That framing set the design problem. The audience spans hardware engineers who know exactly how hard routing is and decision-makers who mainly need to understand the claim, so the site had to be credible to the first group without being opaque to the second.

02The pages

Ten pages at desktop width: the home page, an enterprise page and pricing, an editorial pair of blog index and article template, and the supporting help, contact, terms and 404 pages.

Designing the whole set rather than only the home page is what keeps a marketing site coherent — the legal and error pages are where consistency usually breaks down.

03The InstaDeep design system

I built InstaDeep's design system end to end and still own it — not just the static components, but the motion and interaction that go with them: how a thing enters, what it does while it waits, how it responds to being used.

That second half is the part most systems skip, and it is the part that decides whether a product feels considered or merely assembled. Specifying it once, centrally, is what keeps every surface behaving the same way instead of each screen inventing its own timing.

It is deliberately the least visible work here. Nobody notices a design system that is doing its job; they notice the week it saves when the next page has to exist by Friday.

04The outcome

The site is live at deeppcb.ai, and the work did not stop at launch — we have kept refining the design since.

The current focus is measurement rather than opinion. I run A/B tests in VWO across the whole funnel, from the landing page through to the most-used feature inside the application, so changes ship on evidence of what people actually do rather than on preference or seniority.

DeepPCB itself is InstaDeep's reinforcement-learning router: it places and routes boards with no human in the loop, reports 96% mean completion and DRC-clean output by default, and is used by hardware teams to cut design cycles that used to run for weeks.
