Design is how I think.
Building is how I prove it.

What holds my attention is interaction design — how a thing responds, what it does while it waits, and how it recovers. The happy path is the easy half; the interesting half is everything after it.

I visualise a product as a system before I draw a screen. The structure comes first — what the states are, what can fail, what each surface is allowed to say — and the interface is what that structure looks like once it has to face someone.

Then I build it. A prototype in code answers questions a static frame cannot, and it is the fastest way to find out that a flow which reads well does not feel right. This site is the proof: my Figma, my code, and a test suite that checks its own claims.

Today I'm a senior product designer at InstaDeep, an AI company acquired by BioNTech, working on their AI products and the design system underneath them. Before that I led product design at Steer, and at T-ledger I designed one of Tunisia's first digital wallets, built on blockchain.

Six years, four companies, and one studio of my own — fintech, automotive retail, food delivery, home services and AI.

Based in Tunis — UTC+1, the same working day as Paris and Berlin.

Values I believe in

Craft is most of the job. The rest is how you behave when the work gets ambiguous, late, or contested — these are what I come back to.

  1. 01

    Stay next to the build

    Design that stops at handoff gets built as something else. At Steer I walked the developers through every screen and followed the build; at Konnect the constant back and forth with the CTO is the reason it shipped functional rather than merely drawn.

  2. 02

    Design for the room you are not in

    The InstaDeep system had to survive being used by engineers with no designer present. If a component only works while I am there to explain it, I have not finished it.

  3. 03

    Ask for the criticism early

    A review where nobody disagrees is a review that found nothing. I would rather hear it at the prototype than at the launch, and rather document a disagreement than win it quietly.
